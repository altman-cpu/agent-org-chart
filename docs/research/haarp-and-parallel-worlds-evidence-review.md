# HAARP, similar facilities, and “parallel worlds”: evidence review

**Research date:** 2026-10-24

**Purpose:** Check whether public scientific sources about HAARP and related discoveries provide evidence that parallel worlds exist.

**Conclusion:** The sources reviewed do **not** confirm parallel worlds, and they do not show that HAARP creates, detects, or communicates with them. This is a source review—not a direct measurement or experiment.

## Terms that should not be conflated

- **HAARP** is a ground-based high-frequency radio research facility that studies the Earth's ionosphere and upper atmosphere.
- **Many-worlds** is one interpretation of quantum mechanics. It is not a claim that people can visit, message, or identify alternate versions of themselves.
- **Cosmological multiverse** models propose other cosmic regions or universes. Different models have different proposed tests; they are not the same hypothesis as many-worlds.
- **Alternate timeline / portal / cross-world communication** is a stronger, distinct claim. None of the sources below establishes it.

## Findings

### 1. What HAARP is documented to do

The University of Alaska Fairbanks (UAF) describes HAARP as a high-frequency transmitter for studying the ionosphere. Its FAQ says radio waves heat electrons and create small, temporary perturbations in a limited region so researchers can observe and repeat controlled experiments. It also says induced effects dissipate quickly—reported as less than a second to about ten minutes depending on altitude—and that the transmitted frequencies do not interact with the lower atmospheric layers that produce weather. [UAF HAARP FAQ](https://haarp.gi.alaska.edu/faq)

The FAQ identifies UAF as the operator after the research equipment transferred from the U.S. Air Force in 2015. It describes the work as upper-atmosphere, plasma, radio-propagation, and space-physics research—not cosmology or parallel-world research. [UAF HAARP FAQ](https://haarp.gi.alaska.edu/faq)

### 2. Comparable research does not imply a portal or another universe

EISCAT's Tromsø site operates a high-frequency Heating facility alongside radar instruments. EISCAT says the heater temporarily modifies a small ionospheric region so researchers can test plasma-physics theories and study auroras, radio propagation, and energy transfer. EISCAT describes it as an upper-atmosphere laboratory and says it does not affect Earth's weather or climate. [EISCAT Tromsø site](https://eiscat.se/eiscat-sites/eiscat-tromso-site/)

That is a comparable *kind of ionospheric experiment*. Similarity to HAARP is not evidence of a connection to other universes. In the sources reviewed, neither facility is described as a multiverse experiment.

### 3. What current sources say about parallel-world hypotheses

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes many-worlds as an interpretation of quantum mechanics and notes that there is no experimental evidence favoring collapse over many-worlds. That means the interpretation is debated; it does not amount to an observation of accessible alternate worlds. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Many-Worlds Interpretation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qm-manyworlds/)

A National Geographic overview reports that no direct observation has established a multiverse and that whether some multiverse proposals are empirically testable remains disputed. This is a useful high-level summary, not a substitute for evaluating a specific model and its predictions. [National Geographic: What is the multiverse—and is there evidence?](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/what-is-the-multiverse)

### 4. The ANITA headline is not a HAARP result or a confirmed parallel universe

One 2018 theoretical paper proposed a CPT-symmetric-universe explanation for two unusual upward-going events reported by the Antarctic ANITA experiment. The paper is a proposed interpretation of an anomaly—not a report that another universe was detected. [Anchordoqui et al., arXiv:1803.11554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11554)

A later IceCube Collaboration search in the directions of ANITA neutrino candidates reported no significant matching detection and set upper limits on possible neutrino flux. That result does not disprove every multiverse idea; it does mean the ANITA events do not establish the proposed parallel-universe explanation. [IceCube Collaboration, arXiv:2001.01737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01737)

ANITA is a separate Antarctic particle-astrophysics experiment. It is not HAARP, and the sources reviewed provide no evidence linking ANITA's anomalous events to HAARP.

## Evidence assessment

| Claim | What the reviewed sources support | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| HAARP studies the ionosphere using HF radio | UAF's facility FAQ describes this directly | Supported |
| HAARP's controlled perturbations are observations of another universe | No reviewed facility or research source reports this | Unsupported |
| A multiverse / many-worlds idea exists in theoretical physics | Expert and scientific sources discuss several distinct hypotheses | Established as a research/philosophy topic; not established as an observed fact |
| ANITA discovered a parallel universe | A theoretical interpretation was proposed for unusual events; follow-up IceCube analysis found no significant matching events | Not confirmed |
| Someone can communicate with or travel to another world using HAARP | No evidence in the reviewed sources | Unsupported |

## How to check future claims

For a claim of a “discovery,” look for all of the following before treating it as established:

1. A primary paper or official instrument report, not just a headline or repost.
2. A specific, quantitative prediction that distinguishes the claim from ordinary explanations.
3. Instrument methods, uncertainties, and data sufficient for independent scrutiny.
4. Independent replication or a follow-up measurement.
5. Serious comparison against mundane alternatives, calibration errors, and selection effects.
6. A clear distinction between **an observation**, **a proposed explanation**, and **a confirmed conclusion**.

A surprising signal can be worth investigating without being evidence for the most dramatic explanation. If a specific HAARP campaign, dataset, or claimed discovery is meant, provide its title, date, or primary source and it can be checked separately.
